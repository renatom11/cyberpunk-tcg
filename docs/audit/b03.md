# Batch b03 — `on_play` across the whole pool: sides, counts, optionality and ordering

Cross-cutting pass. The reading unit was one *mechanism* over all 151 cards, not a contiguous block
of `src/cptcg/cards/sets/wnc.py`: for every card that carries an `on_play` (and, where the batch
list named one, an `on_attack` / `on_event` / `Ability` instead), what does the printed text say
about **which side** a prompt may pick from, **how many** it must pick, whether the pick is
**declinable**, and in **what order** the printed sentences run — and does the script agree.

**Headline.** The census closes the column rather than adding to it: across all 151 cards there is
now **exactly one** script that makes a bare printed imperative declinable — `sketchy-ripper`, filed
as `AUD-sketchy-ripper-1` — and **zero** that pick from the wrong side. Both statements are now
mechanical lints in `tests/cards/audit/test_b03.py`, so a *second* card acquiring either shape fails
the suite today instead of waiting for an auditor to be assigned that block.

---

## 1. Why a census and not nineteen readings

The four findings the brief named (`bonnie-and-clyde` lo=0, `unlikely-bond` `optional=True`,
`shattered-memories` "may" auto-taken, `sketchy-ripper` lo=0) are all one shape: *a printed word of
permission, or its absence, not reflected in a bound or a flag.* A per-section reader finds them one
at a time and can never say whether there are four or forty. The question this pass exists to answer
is the second one, so the deliverable is the whole column, plus a lint that keeps it closed.

Three of those four have been fixed while this pass was running (`f449ed5`, `9b1fb41`, `e8a49fc`);
the census below is against the tree at `6ba1af0`.

## 2. Optionality: "may" / "up to" / "any number" versus mandatory

**Majority pattern.** A printed `may`, `up to N` or `any number` is the *only* thing that puts a
decline on the menu. Every one of the 24 cards that prints one of those words carries exactly one of
`optional=True`, `c.maybe(...)`, `c.offer_call_free()`, a `choose_many` / `search_top` with a
literal `lo=0`, or `adjust_up_to` (whose decline is built in — "up to N" includes zero). Every card
that prints a bare imperative carries none of them.

**The departure.** One row, already filed:

| card | printed | code | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sketchy-ripper` | "Reveal a Gear **and add it to your hand**." | `search_top(3, GEAR, **0**, 1)` | lo=0 makes a mandatory add declinable — `AUD-sketchy-ripper-1` |

`tests/cards/audit/test_b03.py::test_no_bare_imperative_is_declinable` is that sentence as a lint
over all `@script` blocks: it reports `sketchy-ripper` and nothing else, and the pinned-open set is
`{"sketchy-ripper"}` so a second card fails it immediately. It has a self-test that plants three
violations, because a guard that passes by finding nothing is the failure mode this workstream is
about.

Rows a reader coming from the four filed findings would be tempted to add, and why they are not
findings:

* `bonnie-and-clyde` — the "may" attaches to the *upgrade* ("You may defeat 2 **instead**"), which
  is `hi`, not `lo`. Already resolved that way upstream.
* `three-mouths-one-desire` — "Add 1 to your hand. **You may add 1 more for each** friendly min
  Gig" is `lo=1, hi=1+len(min_gigs())`. The "may" is the distance between lo and hi, and the script
  has it exactly.
* `heywood-ripperdoc`, `maxtac-av`, `peace-offering` — `optional=True` on each, and each prints
  "You may".
* `we-gotta-live-together` — "Play **up to 2**" is `lo=0, hi=2`. Correct.

## 3. Sides: friendly / rival / unqualified

**Majority pattern.** The set says *friendly* when it means friendly and *rival* when it means
rival, and an unqualified noun means **either player's**. The scripts honour that without exception:
`c.units()` / `c.all_gear()` / `c.gig_values()` for "friendly", `c.rival_units()` /
`c.all_gear(c.rival)` for "rival", and an explicit both-sides comprehension for an unqualified one.

`data/cards/wnc.json`):

The three unqualified nouns in this batch — `heywood-ripperdoc`'s "a Gear",
`dont-fear-the-reaper`'s "a spent Unit", and the "a Gig" of the four Gig cards — each resolve to
**both** players', and each has a sibling that proves the convention is deliberate:
`gilded-maton` prints "a **friendly** Gear" and uses `c.all_gear()`, `detonate` prints "a **rival**
Gear" and uses `c.all_gear(c.rival)`, and `wild-in-the-streets` prints the identical unqualified "a
spent Unit" and builds the identical both-sides list. None of them is a finding, and the temptation
to file one is exactly what the brief warned about.

`tests/cards/audit/test_b03.py::test_a_targeting_prompt_offers_the_side_the_card_names` is that
paragraph as a lint: no script may build candidates from `c.rival_units(` unless its printed text
says "rival", and none from the bare friendly `c.units()` unless its text says "friendly". It
reports one card over the whole pool, `live-with-the-aftermath` ("Each player defeats one of their
Units"), which uses **both** and names neither — the one genuinely two-sided phrasing in the set, so
it is pinned as an exclusion the way the tail-clause lint pins its back-references.

## 4. Counts and ordering

**Counts.** Every "for each" and "up to N" bound in the batch was checked against a board where the
bound binds: `sandayu-oda` spends `value_pairs()` rival Units (lo = hi = n, clamped to what exists),
`three-mouths-one-desire` takes 1 plus one per friendly min Gig, `we-gotta-live-together` 0–2,
`bonnie-and-clyde` 1–2. `adam-smasher-metal-over-meat` ("all other") and `dont-fear-the-reaper`
("all rival") take no prompt at all, which is what "all" means. No disagreement.

**Ordering.** `EffectCtx.choose` *pushes an `AskStep` and returns* — it does not block — so any
statement written after a prompting call runs **before** the answer arrives. That is a real bug
class in this repo (it is why `adjust_up_to`, `choose_gig`, `spend_one` and now `maybe` all grew an
`after=` hook, and why six cards are open under `AUD-tail-clause`). I walked all 61 `on_play`
scripts for it. Exactly two put a second printed effect after a prompt rather than in a hook:
`chrome-reverie` (not this batch) and `floor-it`. See §5.1 for why `floor-it` is not filed.

## 5. Examined, not filed

**5.1 `floor-it` — "Give a rival Unit -1 power this turn. Draw 1."** `temp_power_one` pushes the
prompt and `c.draw(1)` then runs immediately, so the card draws before the player says which rival
Unit loses the power. That is out of printed order, and I could construct no printed-text outcome
that differs: the draw is unconditional, nothing in the pool triggers on a draw, the pending prompt
is still answered before the fight it was played into resolves, and on an empty deck both orders end
the game the same way. The only difference is that the chooser sees one more card before deciding —
real, but not a board state I can assert against the card. Recorded rather than filed. *(The
absence of a continuation is also what makes `floor-it` **right** about the other half of the same
question: with no rival Unit on the board the prompt is skipped and the printed "Draw 1" still
happens. That is a confirmation, and it is in `test_confirmed_b03.py`.)*

**5.2 `take-control` versus `gorilla-arms`.** "A rival Unit steals 1 fewer Gig this turn" is a claim
about every path on which that Unit steals, and `steal_fewer` is read at exactly one site
(`steps.ResolveAttackStep`, the attack-steal count). `gorilla-arms` pushes an extra steal outside
that site. It works out: Gorilla Arms adds a fixed 1 and Take Control subtracts a fixed 1, so the
total is still one fewer than it would have been. No finding. (The *protection* half of the same
question — Gorilla Arms not consulting `stealable` — was filed separately as `AUD-gorilla-arms-1`.)

**5.3 `overwatch-panams-gift`'s `legal=lambda c: bool(c.hand())`.** The ability is not offered with
an empty hand, which narrows the legal action set beyond anything printed. It is unreachable as a
difference: with an empty hand the effect provably does nothing (`discard(1)` over an empty hand
resolves to `[]`, the defeat clause is guarded by `if picks`), and no card in the set reads whether
a *Gear* is spent, so there is no reason to pay 1 €$ for it. Recorded, not filed.
