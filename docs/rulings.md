# Rulings

The official guide leaves some situations unsettled; the Comprehensive Rules (last updated Sep 1, 2026) settled most of them and are cited below as "CR x.y". Rather than guess silently in code, each is
recorded here with a **default** and a corresponding field in `RulesConfig`. The active config is
hashed into every simulation result and replay, so changing a ruling visibly invalidates
comparisons with older runs instead of quietly shifting them.

**The official FAQ now outranks everything below.** `data/faq.txt` holds the published card and
general FAQs verbatim (269 answers over 140 of the 151 cards, plus 25 keyword rulings), and where it
speaks it settles the question outright — not by argument, by the publisher saying so. Three rulings
recorded here as **Uncertain** were decided that way, and are marked *Settled (FAQ)* below. Anything
the FAQ does not reach still falls back to the Comprehensive Rules, as before.

A known defect in that source: the keyword **icons did not survive transcription**, so some answers
read with a gap where a symbol was. Those entries are flagged `icons_missing` and are *not* used as
authority for anything — an icon guessed wrong would freeze the wrong rule into a test. Every FAQ
citation below is to an entry whose meaning does not depend on a missing symbol.

Status legend — **Settled**: the guide implies it strongly enough that the alternative breaks the
game. **Settled (FAQ)**: the published FAQ answers it directly. **Uncertain**: a real coin-flip;
revisit when official clarification lands.

| 043 | May an effect that says "Spend a rival Unit" target a Unit that is **already spent**? | **Yes, as the engine has it** — an already-spent Unit is a legal target and the spend simply does nothing (`ops.spend` returns immediately). Ruling **013** points the other way for BLOCKER, where "spend this Unit" is a *cost* the defender pays and a cost that cannot be paid cannot be chosen; here it is an *effect* choosing a target, and the mainstream convention across trading card games is that a spend/tap effect may name an already-spent permanent. The card text is silent and the distinction between paying a cost and applying an effect is not one this set ever spells out. It matters on exactly one card: Memory Relapse also says "It can't ready until your next turn", so targeting a spent Unit with it is a real play — the rider lands on a Unit that was never actually spent. | 045 | May an ability with **no legal target** be activated? | **As the engine has it: no for a target, yes for a magnitude or a printed condition** — and the set contains a counterexample to the first half. Four abilities carry an availability guard: Hanako *Daughter of the Emperor* ("Swap a friendly Gig with a rival Gig" — needs a die on each side), Panam *Nomad Cavalry* ("Move a Gear from this Legend to an unequipped friendly Unit"), Overwatch — Panam's Gift ("Discard 1. Defeat a spent rival Unit…" — needs a card to discard) and Panam *Strength Through Family* (guarded by the once-per-turn Call rule rather than by a target). Against them stands **Padre — Man of the Cross**, which prints the same two-sided shape ("⊡: Set a player's Gig to the same value as another player's Gig"), carries no guard, and is offered with no dice on the board at all — spent, resolved, nothing happens. Left as the engine has it, with the Padre counterexample recorded rather than explained away. | — | Uncertain |
| 046 | When two of a player's cards trigger on the **same event**, who chooses the order they resolve in? | **The controller of the triggers chooses.** Three general FAQ entries say so, and between them they cover all three populations this ruling said any answer would have to reach: *"When I have mulitple [⊡] effects that activate and go into pending at the same time.
